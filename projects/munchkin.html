Munchkin

A robotic feeding assistant that tracks your mouth and swaps its own utensils.

Fall 2024 Olin College Munchkin LLC, Massachusetts
SAG Grant recipient SEED Grant recipient 4-DOF robotic arm Real-time face tracking Automatic utensil changing V3 in testing

V1 system demo: the arm scoops food and brings it to the user's tracked mouth position.

Our assistive feeding device combines computer vision, robotics and embedded systems into an affordable solution for individuals with limited upper-body mobility.

The system uses real-time mouth tracking to position food at the optimal location, giving users control over their own feeding process. Built with accessibility in mind, the entire system costs under $600 and is fully open-source so other teams can replicate it.

The project was awarded the Olin College SAG Grant for prototyping support and the Olin College SEED Grant for continued development. We have since formed Munchkin LLC, licensed in Massachusetts, to support structured user testing at Briarwood Rehabilitation & Healthcare Center and future development.

V1 – original design: decisions and trade-offs

We explored several design approaches before landing on our final solution. Our initial concepts included a stationary arm with a rotating base, a gantry-style system, and a jointed arm similar to industrial robots.

01 · Robotic arm configuration

  • Options: 4-DOF articulated arm, 4-DOF SCARA-style, 2-DOF pan-tilt mechanism.
  • Our choice: 4-DOF articulated arm with base rotation, shoulder, elbow and wrist joints.
  • Rationale: we chose the 4-DOF articulated configuration for maximum workspace flexibility and natural motion. The additional wrist joint (vs. 3-DOF) allows proper utensil orientation regardless of approach angle, critical for smooth feeding. While more complex than simpler designs, the improved dexterity justified the added servos and control complexity.

02 · Mouth tracking approach

  • Options: MediaPipe Face Mesh, OpenCV Haar Cascades, custom CNN model, depth camera only.
  • Our choice: MediaPipe Face Mesh with a RealSense depth camera.
  • Rationale: MediaPipe provides 468 facial landmarks at 30 fps with excellent accuracy on a Raspberry Pi 4, and combines well with RealSense depth data for precise 3D positioning. Pre-trained models eliminated weeks of custom ML training, and the open-source implementation made debugging straightforward. Haar Cascades were too inaccurate, and custom CNNs would require extensive datasets we didn't have.

03 · Computing platform

  • Options: Raspberry Pi, Arduino + OpenCV on a laptop, Jetson Nano, microcontroller with cloud processing.
  • Our choice: Raspberry Pi 4B (4 GB RAM).
  • Rationale: the Raspberry Pi 4 offers the right balance of compute power and cost ($62 vs. $200+ for a Jetson). Its quad-core ARM processor handles MediaPipe at 30 fps while managing servo control and safety monitoring. Built-in USB 3.0 for the camera, GPIO for the safety buttons and strong community support made development smooth. Arduino lacked ML capability, and cloud processing introduced unacceptable latency for safety-critical feeding motions.

Why latency matters

The Pi 4 responds to a tracked mouth movement in about 45 ms, and it does so consistently, 24/7, without fatigue. Average human reaction time is 200–300 ms. That consistent low latency is what lets Munchkin follow the mouth in real time without noticeable lag, which is critical for safe, reliable feeding assistance.

System architecture

The V1 electronics split into three groups: inputs (a depth camera, two emergency stops and two isolated power rails), processing on the Raspberry Pi (face tracking, inverse kinematics, safety monitoring and serial servo control through a BusLinker board) and outputs (four daisy-chained serial-bus servos and the utensil end effector).
