Motorized Beach Wheelchair

Independent beach access for under $1,500.

2024 Avalon Beach Patrol, NJ Westtown School · Scientific Research (Adv)
Fusion 360 PVC frame Waterproof motors LiFePO4 batteries Under $1,500

This project set out to improve beach accessibility with a cost-effective motorized beach wheelchair. Working with Avalon Beach Patrol in New Jersey, who provided the original manual wheelchair as a model, I designed and built a motorized version that significantly improves user independence while staying affordable.

Design process

Initial research and planning

I began by analysing the existing manual beach wheelchair donated by Avalon Beach Patrol and identifying its key limitations:

  • Difficulty navigating sand, and the user's dependence on caregivers.
  • Set a target budget of $1,500 to keep the design accessible.
  • Conducted material research focused on durability and water resistance.

CAD design phase

I fully modelled the wheelchair in Fusion 360, focusing on:

  • Custom mounts for the motors and battery systems.
  • Component placement optimised for weight distribution.
  • Detailed assembly instructions.
  • Waterproofing considerations integrated throughout the design.

Build process

Frame construction

  • Cut and assembled the PVC frame to the CAD specifications.
  • Used marine-grade materials for durability.
  • Reinforced the joints for structural integrity.

Motorization system

  • Installed dual waterproof electric motors.
  • Integrated 12V 50Ah LiFePO4 batteries for extended use.
  • Added emergency stop functionality.
  • Implemented a joystick control system.

Safety features

  • Waterproof electronics enclosure.
  • User safety belt system.
  • Water sensor integration.
  • Redundant emergency stops.

Key improvements over the traditional design

Independent user control via joystick, plus:

  • Enhanced stability on sandy terrain.
  • Waterproof electrical systems.
  • Improved maneuverability: 9.0/10 vs 2.0/10 in testing.
  • Significant cost savings and additional motorized features compared to commercial options ($1,500 vs $3,000+).

Project impact

This project shows that accessible design can be both affordable and effective. The motorized beach wheelchair improved user independence while cutting costs by 50% compared to commercial alternatives. Most importantly, it gives beach access to people who would otherwise need constant caregiver assistance, significantly enhancing their beach experience and autonomy.

Research poster

The poster presented the project for Westtown School's Scientific Research (Adv) course, asking whether motorization and upgrades improve user independence on beach wheelchairs. Six testers compared the manual and motorized chairs in sand pit and lake environments. The new wheelchair took 55.6% of total rating points against 44.4% for the old one, with the biggest gains in maneuverability (9.0 vs 2.0), stability and safety (8.0 vs 3.0) and speed control (9.0 vs 4.0); the old chair still scored higher on maintenance (8.0 vs 7.0) and durability (6.0 vs 5.0).
