At AT Dev Inc, a medical device startup, I developed and prototyped assistive technology for people with physical disabilities and post-surgical rehabilitation needs. I contributed to FDA approval processes, precision machining, and the design of a new leg brace fastening system that combines 3D scanning with guitar tuning peg mechanics.

Main project: a custom leg brace with a new fastening system

I designed and developed a first-of-its-kind leg brace attachment system that pairs 3D scanning technology with guitar tuning peg mechanics for a superior fit and ease of use.

Design innovation

The attachment system uses a dual-sided approach, optimised for both patient comfort and clinical precision:

  • 3D-scanned side: a 3D scan of the patient's leg creates a custom-moulded surface for a perfect anatomical fit.
  • Guitar tuning peg side: a mechanism that needs minimal force to tighten around the leg.
  • Individual finger adjustment: four independent "fingers", each individually adjustable by the physician for precise fit customisation.
  • String-based mechanism: a string threaded through the fingers; pulling it makes the fingers wrap around and close, distributing pressure evenly.

FDA approval and compliance

I contributed to the FDA approval application by making sure the products exceeded regulatory safety requirements:

  • Conducted rigorous force testing on brace components to verify safety margins.
  • Ensured the factor of safety exceeded FDA requirements by a substantial margin.
  • Performed finite element analysis (FEA) in SolidWorks to validate structural integrity.
  • Documented testing procedures and results for the regulatory submission.
  • Machined test fixtures and validation equipment.

  • Tested parts under load to validate the FEA predictions.
  • Iterated designs based on manufacturing feedback and test results.

What I learned

This internship gave me exposure to the complete medical device development cycle, from initial concept to market-ready product. Working at a startup taught me how to operate with limited resources, move quickly while maintaining quality, and balance innovation with regulatory compliance.

Seeing the product I helped develop actually being used by patients reinforced the importance of user-centred design. Every design decision had real-world implications for someone's mobility and quality of life, which pushed me to be more thoughtful about ergonomics, ease of use and reliability.

I also learned how startups operate differently from larger companies: team members wear multiple hats, rapid prototyping and iteration matter, and investor relationships and client feedback directly shape product development. Those insights have been invaluable for understanding entrepreneurship and product development at every scale.
