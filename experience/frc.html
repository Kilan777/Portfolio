Engineering Notebook 2024 (PDF)

In the 2024 season (CRESCENDO) I served as Team Captain, Lead Builder and Robot Operator. We designed and built a highly competitive robot that acquired and scored game pieces with remarkable consistency, built around a four-bar intake mechanism of my design with sensor-based detection and automated controls. Under my leadership we won multiple awards including Engineering Excellence, competed at the World Championship and ranked in the top 3% globally.

Leadership and responsibilities

  • Team Captain: managed the overall robot design and build process, coordinated between sub-teams, led daily build meetings and progress reviews.
  • Lead Builder: developed the four-bar intake mechanism with sensor-based detection and automated controls.
  • Robot Operator: operated the robot during competitions with consistent high performance.
  • Documentation Lead: created a detailed engineering notebook covering the entire development process, setting new standards for the team's technical documentation.
  • Mentorship: trained and mentored new team members in CAD, machining and design processes.

Key mechanical systems

Four-bar intake mechanism

Developed through seven major design iterations (V1–V7): an intake with ground pickup capability and automatic collision response.

  • Ground pickup: automatic wall detection and sensor-based game-piece tracking.
  • Virtual four-bar geometry: stores compactly inside the frame perimeter and extends 12 inches for collection.
  • Self-protecting design: collision response system using 1/4" polycarbonate arms with chain drive.
  • Sensor integration: beam-break sensors for automated detection and consistent piece acquisition.
  • Optimized performance: 10 polyurethane belts (5 per side) for reliable note collection at any robot speed.

Adjustable shooter

Designed through multiple prototyping iterations to achieve consistent long-range shooting with automated targeting.

  • Dual flywheel design: two rows of wheels with independent speed control for ball spin.
  • Optimized compression: 1.5" compression distance found through extensive testing.
  • Centered pivot point: low center of gravity for balanced operation.
  • 3D-printed hood: custom geometry for the optimal ball trajectory.
  • Vision integration: Limelight-based targeting with distance calculation.

Elevator and climber

  • Two-stage design: inner rails sliding on outer rails using bearings.
  • Shooter mount: through-bore encoder for precise rotation control.
  • Improved stability: redesigned after the first competition with C-channel support and chain drive.
  • Maximum height: reaches the amp and trap scoring positions.

26" × 26" swerve drive base

  • Compact design: minimal footprint while fitting all subsystems.
  • Low center of gravity: belly pan positioned as low as possible.
  • Lightweight construction: 1/16" box tubing on the inner rails with strategic weight reduction.
  • Easy mounting: end caps for clean box connections without angle brackets.

Engineering Notebook 2023 (PDF)

During the 2023 season (CHARGED UP) I served as Team Captain, Lead Builder and Robot Operator. Our robot "BAMI" featured a dual-purpose game-piece handling system that could manipulate both cones and cubes at multiple scoring heights. It used a swerve drive chassis for exceptional maneuverability, a two-rotation-point arm with inverse kinematics, and a V4 grabber with Sorbothane grip pads. Through extensive prototyping and iteration we built a competition-ready robot with innovative mechanical design and autonomous capability.

Key mechanical systems

Swerve drive chassis

A highly maneuverable drivetrain providing omnidirectional movement for precise field positioning.

  • 27" × 27" frame: compact design optimized for charge-station balancing.
  • Steel electronics board: custom-machined steel board for a low center of gravity.
  • 80-degree tip resistance: could tilt up to 80 degrees without falling over.
  • Countersunk holes: flat-head screws prevent catching on the charge station.
  • Strategic cutouts: component access without removing the board.
  • Enhanced maneuverability: full 360° rotation and translation.

Image placeholder: 2023 swerve drive chassis (Engineering Notebook 2023, pages 24–25).

Two-rotation-point arm (shoulder + elbow)

A double-jointed articulated arm enabling precise game-piece placement from ground level to the high scoring positions.

  • Dual-joint design: shoulder and elbow rotation points for maximum reach and flexibility.
  • Chain-driven actuation: shoulder motors mounted low in the frame to optimize the center of gravity.
  • Through-bore encoders: precise angle monitoring for both the shoulder and elbow joints.
  • Inverse kinematics: software positioning from x, y coordinate input.
  • Lightweight construction: 1×2 box tubing with strategic material reduction.
  • Upgraded chain: #35 chain on the shoulder joint (upgraded from #25) for improved reliability.
  • Multiple scoring heights: reaches all three grid levels plus ground pickup.

Image placeholder: 2023 double-jointed arm prototype (Engineering Notebook 2023, page 19).

V4 grabber

Advanced through four major design iterations to create the final dual-purpose gripper for both cones and cubes.

  • Sheet-metal construction: the V4 grabber is machined from sheet metal for durability and precision.
  • Sorbothane grip pads: superior grip material replacing the initial wheel-based design.
  • Dual pneumatic pistons: linear actuation providing variable grip strength.
  • 3D-printed mounts: custom geometry for component integration.
  • Pressure-sensitive design: handles both game-piece types with the appropriate force.
  • Compact paddles: smaller paddles on V4 reduce cube over-gripping and prevent sticking.
  • Sensor integration: automated control for consistent piece acquisition.

Image placeholder: 2023 V4 grabber with Sorbothane pads (Engineering Notebook 2023, page 30).

Over-the-bumper intake

Developed through seven major iterations to create a reliable floor-collection system for both cones and cubes.

  • 12.5-degree opening: wide capture range for forgiving piece acquisition.
  • Dual-wheel design: two-layer wheels providing grip without launching pieces.
  • Virtual four-bar mechanism: stores compactly within the frame perimeter and extends for collection.
  • 1/8" polycarbonate arms: upgraded thickness for reduced flex during operation.
  • Constant-force springs: assisted deployment and retraction.
  • Automated detection: sensor-based control for automatic piece handling.
  • Natural cone orientation: uses gravity to orient the bottom-heavy cones correctly.

Image placeholder: 2023 over-the-bumper intake system.

AprilTag vision system

Computer vision for autonomous and driver-assisted precision alignment during scoring.

  • AprilTag detection: visual fiducial markers for grid position identification.
  • Distance targeting: area calculation (getTA()) targeting 3.4% for the optimal scoring distance.
  • Centering algorithm: x-axis offset (getTX()) for precise horizontal alignment.
  • Automated positioning: driver-assist features for consistent scoring.
  • Autonomous integration: vision-based scoring during the autonomous period.

Engineering Notebook 2022 (PDF)

As a key member of Team 1391 during the 2022 Rapid React season, I contributed to our robot "Gustavo," which featured new approaches to shooting and climbing: an unlimited-rotation turret with a slip ring for continuous vision tracking, a three-stage climbing elevator capable of traversing to the high bar, and a thirty-wheel indexer with color-sensor integration. These systems enabled consistent autonomous performance and helped carry the team all the way to the World Championship in Houston, Texas.

Image placeholder: 2022 robot "Gustavo," full view (Engineering Notebook 2022, page 3).

Key mechanical systems

Unlimited-rotation turret with vision tracking

A shooting platform with continuous 360° rotation and automated vision-based targeting.

  • Slip-ring design: the Limelight camera is powered through a slip ring, enabling unlimited continuous rotation.
  • Vision-based auto-targeting: retroreflective tape detection with real-time angle calculations.
  • NEO motor control: built-in encoder for precise angle positioning and tracking.
  • One-to-one gear ratio: the Limelight rotates synchronously with the turret for accurate targeting.
  • Four-flywheel system: consistent ball propulsion with variable speed control based on distance.
  • 3D-printed and CNC hood: custom geometry for the optimal ball trajectory (a combination of CNC cutting and 3D printing).
  • Automated distance calculation: RoboRIO integration for dynamic power adjustment based on range.

Image placeholder: 2022 unlimited-rotation turret with slip ring (Engineering Notebook 2022, page 2).

Three-stage climbing system

A belt-and-piston elevator mechanism engineered for traversal-bar ascent with anti-swing features.

  • Belt-and-piston design: enables rapid bar-to-bar traversal to the high and traversal bars.
  • Anti-swing mechanism: a piston pulls the robot closer to lower its center of mass during the climb.
  • Dual hook system: fixed and moving hooks with precision-engineered tilt angles.
  • Spring-loaded fixed hooks: let the bar slide through during transitions between stages.
  • Aluminum channel construction: integrated belts and bearings for smooth, reliable operation.
  • Multi-process manufacturing: components fabricated on the CNC router, mill, 3D printer and lathe.
  • Four-hook configuration: redundant hooks for secure attachment at each climbing stage.

Image placeholder: 2022 three-stage climber action shot (Engineering Notebook 2022, page 2).

Intelligent cargo management: thirty-wheel indexer

An indexing system with integrated color sensors for automated cargo sorting and ball tracking.

  • 30-wheel design: precise cargo control and guidance from intake to flywheels.
  • Integrated color sensors: real-time cargo tracking and alliance-color identification.
  • Automated enemy rejection: the system automatically reverses to eject wrong-colored cargo.
  • Dynamic speed adjustment: flywheel speed varies with cargo type and condition.
  • Position monitoring: tracks the exact cargo location within the robot.
  • Driver feedback: visual indicators give operators real-time cargo status.
  • Consistent feed rate: ensures proper timing between intake, indexing and shooting.

Image placeholder: 2022 thirty-wheel indexer with color sensors (Engineering Notebook 2022, page 1).

Over-the-bumper intake

A dual-arm pneumatic collection system with vision-assisted ball recognition and driver-assist features.

  • Pneumatic polycarbonate arms: compliant yet durable construction for reliable collection.
  • Lower pivot point: the arms pivot away from impacts, providing collision protection.
  • Active wheel collection: powered intake wheels on the arm fronts for rapid cargo acquisition.
  • Secondary Limelight: front-facing camera for automated ball recognition and tracking.
  • Driver-assist features: auto-rotation to center on the nearest cargo for easier collection.
  • Five-ball autonomous: consistent autonomous operation without precise starting alignment.

Tank drive with mixed wheel configuration

  • West Coast drive base: proven drivetrain design for reliable field performance.
  • Mixed wheel configuration: four Versa wheels with two front omni wheels for enhanced turning.
  • Enhanced rotation: omni wheels ease the turning radius while maintaining straight-line speed.
  • Single-speed gearbox: simple but effective drive system optimized for the game.
  • Vision integration: works with the dual-Limelight setup for collection and shooting.
  • CNC-manufactured components: precision parts from the CNC mill and router for durability.

Key learnings

Three seasons of FRC changed how I approach engineering problems. The six-week build season forces rapid iteration and decision-making under pressure: there is no time to perfect every detail in CAD when you need a working prototype to test. It taught me to build physical mock-ups early, fail fast, and iterate on real-world performance rather than theoretical perfection.

Leading 30+ students across mechanical, electrical, programming and business sub-teams showed me that technical excellence alone does not win competitions. Communication between subsystems, clear documentation and a shared understanding of design constraints matter as much as clever mechanisms. When our intake team could not coordinate with the programmers, even the best hardware failed.

Competing at the World Championship and earning multiple Engineering Excellence awards confirmed that thorough documentation and design process matter to judges and clients alike. The engineering notebooks I created became team standards because they forced us to articulate not just what we built, but why each design decision was made and how we validated it.

Most importantly, FRC taught me that setbacks on the field are inevitable: mechanisms break, sensors fail and alliance partners have off matches. The teams that succeed can diagnose problems in the pits, make field repairs under time pressure and adapt strategy to what is actually working. That resilience has proven invaluable in every engineering project since.
